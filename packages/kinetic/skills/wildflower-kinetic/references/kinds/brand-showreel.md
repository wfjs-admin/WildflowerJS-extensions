# A brand piece or a showreel

A piece about who someone is or what they have made: a studio's reel, a
brand film, a launch sting. Read with `../craft.md`.

## The intake, added

- What should the viewer feel about the brand at the end, in a word or two?
- What work or claims are shown, and in what order of importance?
- Is there a track, and is it to be cut to its beat?

## For this kind

- **The identity system leads**: the brand's colors, type and marks,
  applied consistently, with the default style only where the brand is
  silent.
- **Each piece of work gets a moment that does it justice**: shown large,
  held long enough to see, with its name and one line.
- **Rhythm cut to the music** where there is a track: scene changes on the
  bar, accents on the beat. Without a track, choose a pulse anyway and land
  on it, on whole frames. (Saul Bass, cut to Bernstein's count.)
- **Transitions transform.** One scene becomes the next (a line becomes an
  eye, a shape opens into a room) instead of cutting, and each change can be
  said in a sentence. (Ordinary Folk's School of Motion manifesto, which has
  no hard cut in 110 s; Headspace by Buck.)
- **One palette unifies.** Scenes in different manners hold together through
  one palette applied throughout. (Ordinary Folk.)
- **Close where you opened.** The closing motif echoes the opening one, and
  the end card holds 3 to 4 s. (Ordinary Folk; Buck's Azure film.)
- **Expression is a dial**, set once from the brief and kept for the whole
  piece; the default style sits at the calm end. (DIA's A-Trak identity.)
- **Calm, then a burst.** Passages of a few seconds of gentle movement
  between short bursts, closing quietly. (Ordinary Folk, measured.)
