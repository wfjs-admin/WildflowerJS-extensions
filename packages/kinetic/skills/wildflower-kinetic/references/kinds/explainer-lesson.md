# An explainer or a lesson

A piece that teaches: how something works, why something happens, or a
lesson the learner steps through. Read with `../craft.md`, and for a lesson
`style.md`, "Interactive pieces".

## The intake, added

- What should the viewer or learner be able to do or explain at the end?
- What do they know already, and what is new to them?
- Is it a video, a lesson with steps, or both?

## For this kind

- **One idea per step or scene**, built on the one before; a new term is
  shown with what it names.
- **Show, then name**: the picture first, the word on it once the eye is
  there.
- **In a lesson, the learner acts**: each step gives them something to
  move, try or answer, and the scene answers what they did; an opening step
  that orients and a closing step that sums up are the exceptions.
- **A lesson waits for the learner**: each step holds until they press Next
  (`style.md`, "The flow").
- **The point reads with no input.** Nothing the message needs is hidden
  behind a control, a hover or a later step; the frame says it before the
  learner touches anything. (Archie Tse, The New York Times.)
- **Orient first.** The first step shows what the subject is and where we
  are, before any detail. (The *Game of Thrones* titles' makers.)
- **Rest outweighs motion.** An explainer that carries text is near still
  for at least half its running time (`../craft.md`, "Time"). (Buck's
  Microsoft Azure explainer, measured: 62%.)
- **Moving text past 5 s can be paused**: the lesson band's Play and Pause
  covers it. (WCAG 2.2.2.)
- **The model drives the motion.** Anything that behaves by a rule (a
  pendulum, a voltage, a refrigerant's temperature) is computed from its
  equation, with real values and units, never shaped by hand, so the
  animation cannot contradict the science.
- **Show the working where the audience can follow it**: the key equation
  with its values on screen for a class, and the sources for the facts on a
  last card or step. Look the facts up; that is expected. List only sources
  actually read for this piece, never one recalled from memory, and where
  nothing could be looked up, the card says the facts are unchecked.
- **The learner cannot catch your mistakes, so check before you present.**
  The person asking may be the student. Before finishing, check every claim,
  term and value against established sources, run the model at known cases
  (rest, a peak, a limit) and compare with the published values, and fix
  anything that disagrees. Where the piece simplifies (a timescale stretched,
  a ratio rounded), say so in the piece itself, in a line the learner sees.
